Oral Hygiene & Prevention 5 Questions
Keeping your teeth and gums healthy every day
No. Scaling safely removes plaque and tartar from teeth and below the gum line. It doesn't damage enamel or create gaps — the sensation of gaps after scaling is simply because built-up tartar is no longer present. Some temporary sensitivity may occur, but it usually subsides as gums heal and reattach to the teeth.
Scaling can remove surface (extrinsic) stains but not fluorosis, which is an internal (intrinsic) stain. Fluorosis may require treatments like bleaching, veneers, or crowns for cosmetic improvement. Our cosmetic dentistry team can assess the best option for your case.
Brush your teeth twice daily with proper technique, rinse after meals, floss regularly, and schedule routine check-ups with your dentist. Avoid excessive sugary foods and drinks, and drink plenty of water. Consistency in your oral hygiene routine is key to preventing most dental problems.
Use a soft or medium-bristled toothbrush with a small head to reach all areas comfortably. Choose a non-abrasive fluoride toothpaste appropriate for your needs (sensitivity, whitening, etc.) and replace your toothbrush every three months or sooner if the bristles are frayed.
Tooth sensitivity can result from enamel wear due to acidic foods or over-brushing, tooth decay, exposed tooth roots caused by gum recession, or hairline cracks in the teeth. Sensitivity deserves a professional evaluation to identify the root cause and determine the right treatment.

Implants, Missing Teeth & Check-ups 3 Questions
Replacing teeth and staying on top of your dental health
Yes. Replacing extracted teeth helps maintain proper bite alignment and prevents adjacent teeth from drifting or shifting into the gap over time. Missing teeth can also affect jawbone health, facial structure, chewing ability, and speech. Options include implants, bridges, and dentures.
Dental implants restore both the appearance and full functionality of missing teeth. They provide a stable, natural-looking and feeling solution that preserves jawbone health, improves confidence, and enhances overall quality of life. With proper care, implants can last a lifetime — making them the gold standard for tooth replacement.
Visiting your dentist every six months is recommended for routine check-ups, professional cleaning, and preventive care. Regular visits allow early detection of potential issues before they become costly or painful. Patients with gum disease, high cavity risk, or ongoing treatments may need to visit more frequently.
